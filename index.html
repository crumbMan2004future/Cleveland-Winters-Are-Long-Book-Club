📚 BOOK OF THE MONTH 📚

STONER J. WILLIAMS

Stoner

by John Williams (1965)

William Stoner is a Missouri farm boy who goes off to the university to study agriculture, and instead falls head over heels for literature. This quiet little novel didn't sell much when it first came out, but decades later readers rediscovered it and it became a surprise international bestseller. It's proof that good books can wait for us!

✏️ TEACHER'S NOTE

Early in the book, Stoner's English professor reads Shakespeare's Sonnet 73 to the class and asks him what it means, and it changes his whole life! Here it is so you can see what all the fuss is about:

That time of year thou mayst in me behold When yellow leaves, or none, or few, do hang Upon those boughs which shake against the cold, Bare ruin'd choirs, where late the sweet birds sang. In me thou see'st the twilight of such day As after sunset fadeth in the west, Which by and by black night doth take away, Death's second self, that seals up all in rest. In me thou see'st the glowing of such fire That on the ashes of his youth doth lie, As the death-bed whereon it must expire, Consum'd with that which it was nourish'd by. This thou perceiv'st, which makes thy love more strong, To love that well which thou must leave ere long.

Things to think about while you read (no spoilers, promise!)

  1. When Stoner can't answer his professor's question about the sonnet, why do you think that moment hits him so hard?
  2. Have you ever had a moment that sent your life off in a different direction?
  3. John Williams writes in a very plain, quiet style. Does it work for you?
